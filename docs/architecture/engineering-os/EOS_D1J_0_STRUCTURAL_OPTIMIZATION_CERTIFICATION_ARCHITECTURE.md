EOS-D1J-0 structural optimization certification architecture

This phase defines the governed architecture for D1J Structural Optimization Certification. It does not execute an optimization algorithm, a solver, or candidate generation. It does not select a design, mutate a design model, or make a certification claim.

The post-D1I canonical roadmap authorized this phase. The identifier did not exist before that roadmap decision. D1I remains closed at COMPLETE_BOUNDED_WITH_LIMITATIONS and STRUCTURAL_DIGITAL_TWIN_BOUNDED_RUNTIME_IMPLEMENTED. D1G remains the read-only SPACE GASS offline connector and is not a certified live solver.

Existing foundation

D1J reuses the optimization-intelligence study, variable, objective, constraint, alternative, run, Pareto comparison, immutable manifest, and fingerprintCanonical conventions. It reuses lifecycle cost as quantity times an approved rate and carbon from a governed emission factor. It reuses D1A and D1F identity, D1B jurisdiction and standard binding, D1D and D1E checks by reference, D1G analysis references, and D1I historical state references. Formal design approval stays in the existing engineering-review workflow. No parallel generic optimization platform, check engine, asset identity, unit system, or capability manifest is created.

Optimization problem and baseline

An optimization problem is not a structural model, an analysis model, or a design candidate. It pins tenant, workspace, project, structural system, declared scope, baseline, jurisdiction and standard bindings, variable set, objective set, constraint set, evaluation profile, method policy, human authority, limitations, version, fingerprint, and provenance. Scope must be declared. Full-project scope is not inferred.

The baseline is an exact immutable reference. Optimization does not mutate it, float it to latest, or promote its authority. A draft baseline stays a draft.

Design variables

A variable definition names its target, parameter, type, dimensional quantity and unit, domain, bound class, governed source, applicability, version, and fingerprint. Categories are discrete catalogue, bounded continuous, integer, enumeration, boolean, and governed configuration choice. They map onto the existing optimization variable types and do not replace them. An unsourced domain is not allowed. AI cannot invent a governing variable or bound. A missing dimensional unit is not inferred. A search bound is not an engineering limit. A variable set is an immutable fingerprint of those definitions.

Objectives

An objective pins quantity, unit, explicit direction, governed source, authority, scope, version, and fingerprint. Direction is MINIMIZE, MAXIMIZE, or TARGET and is not inferred from the name. Cost uses quantity times an approved rate. Carbon uses a governed factor. AI cannot originate either. An objective set is an immutable fingerprint of its objectives. No default weights, default priority, default normalization, or universal best-design score exist. A later weight set must itself be governed and fingerprinted, and it does not replace raw objective values.

Constraints and feasibility

A constraint is not an objective. It references an existing engineering check and a D1B binding. Jurisdiction is not inferred. Requiredness is REQUIRED, CONDITIONAL, or a governed NOT_APPLICABLE. Unknown applicability is not NOT_APPLICABLE. The required-check set is immutable for the declared scope.

Feasibility is FEASIBLE, INFEASIBLE, or UNDETERMINED. FEASIBLE requires every applicable required check to be identified, current, and PASS. Absence of a failed check is not FEASIBLE. A missing, stale, invalid, unsupported, or unknown required check is not PASS and is not FEASIBLE. A definitive failed required check is INFEASIBLE. An objective value cannot offset a failed constraint or an UNDETERMINED feasibility. A complete evaluation is not feasibility. Feasible is not optimal. Feasible is not certified.

Check and analysis references pin exact identity, version, authority, validation, and fingerprint. They do not float to latest and do not promote analysis authority. A failed or nonconverged analysis cannot support a FEASIBLE candidate. Fixture evaluation is not live solver validation and is not certification.

Candidates, comparison, and Pareto

A candidate pins the problem, baseline, parent where applicable, variable assignments, origin, evaluation refs, and provenance. Origins may later be human-defined, deterministic enumeration, an optimization method, or another governed import. None of those origins runs in this phase. An evaluated assignment is not mutated in place. A candidate is not a selected, approved, issued, code-compliant, or certified design.

Comparison requires the same problem, baseline, objective set, constraint set, jurisdiction and standard binding, evaluation profile, and compatible objective units. Cross-problem dominance is prohibited. Different standard bindings are not directly comparable. Unlike objective units are not compared by name.

Pareto dominance, when later evaluated, applies only to comparison-eligible FEASIBLE candidates: no worse on every shared objective under its explicit direction, and strictly better on at least one. No new engineering formula and no weighting are introduced. The existing optimization-intelligence comparison remains the generic mechanism and is not executed here. UNDETERMINED candidates stay in a separate incomplete set. INFEASIBLE candidates are retained. A non-dominated candidate is not approved. A single-objective minimum is not an approved design. A weighted ranking is not approval.

Human review and certification evidence

Review and selection are explicit records. Final selection requires human authority. AI and the optimizer cannot govern it. Selection history is append-only. Human rationale does not change objective values. Selection is not formal design approval and is not software engineer validation. Approval remains the existing engineering-review workflow.

Certification evidence is EOS process evidence for a structural optimization study. It cites an immutable manifest that pins problem, baseline, variables, objectives, constraints, method, run, evaluations, selection, and analysis or check fingerprints. It is not professional certification, regulatory approval, solver certification, or a certified design. Complete evidence is not a certified design. The solver readiness on that evidence remains NOT_CERTIFIED until the D1G prerequisite is actually met. No D1G validation phase is invented.

Readiness and next phase

Readiness layers are architecture ready, fixture-test ready, pilot runtime ready, live-solver runtime ready, engineer validated, conformance validated, and certification eligible. This phase makes architecture ready only. Fixture tests of the contracts are permitted and are not a runtime claim.

The later sequence is problem and baseline and variable foundation, then objective and constraint and feasibility references, then candidate lineage and comparison composition, then certification-evidence readiness, then closeout. Only the first of those may proceed next, and it still must not generate candidates, evaluate objectives, execute a solver, select a design, or claim certification. Solver-backed evaluation and certification eligibility remain blocked on a certified D1G solver. Engineer validation and conformance validation are separate later gates.

Boundaries that stay closed

D1J may cite D1I and must not mutate it. Condition state does not reduce capacity. Telemetry is not a control loop. Design models are not mutated. Autonomous generative design is not implemented. AI cannot declare feasibility while a required check is missing. Cross-project and cross-tenant references are not allowed. No employee surveillance or person profiling is introduced. D1K, D2, and D3 are not started. The structural pack is not complete.
