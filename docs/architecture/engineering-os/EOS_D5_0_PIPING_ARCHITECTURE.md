# EOS-D5-0 Piping Architecture

## Purpose

D5 is the Piping engineering discipline inside Engineering OS. Phase EOS-D5-0-PIPING-ARCHITECTURE-CANONICALIZATION defines Piping scope, ownership, objects, design-condition and specification context, component and connection context, routing and fabrication context, support and stress evidence context, inspection and test context, authority, currentness, interfaces to Structural, Civil, Geotechnical, and Mechanical, future interface contracts for Process, Electrical, and Instrumentation and Control, the future D9 source contract, and the later implementation sequence. It does not record a Piping object and it does not size, route, or stress-analyze pipe.

The Mechanical track remains complete with the classification COMPLETE_BOUNDED_WITH_LIMITATIONS and a runtime count of eleven. Civil and Geotechnical remain complete with the same classification and runtime count. This phase does not reopen D1, D2, D3, or D4. D1 structural pack complete remains no. D1N stays closed. D5 runtime stays at zero.

## Ownership

D5 owns bounded Piping engineering truth and governance context for plant and project piping systems that the canonical project assigns to Piping. That includes piping system identity, line identity, line and service context, piping service classification, design-condition context, material specification and class context, nominal size and dimensional context when explicitly supplied or adopted, pipe and component context, fittings, flanges, valves, specialties, branches and connections, equipment and nozzle connection context on the Piping side, routing and layout context, arrangement constraints, insulation coating and tracing context where Piping owns the requirement, support and restraint requirement context, flexibility and stress-analysis context and evidence, fabrication spool and isometric context, weld and joint context, examination and NDE context, pressure leak and test context, requirements, assumptions, engineering decisions, evidence, discipline interfaces, review and validation, currentness, lineage, and a future D9 source contract.

Governed engineering context is not calculation runtime. Naming a design pressure, a route constraint, a support requirement, or a stress-analysis case does not calculate, optimize, validate, or approve it.

## Scope

In scope are plant and project piping systems and Piping-owned records covered by the canonical project Piping discipline.

Not automatically in scope are Process engineering, Mechanical equipment design, Structural support design, Civil underground and site design, Geotechnical engineering, Electrical engineering, Instrumentation and Control design, pressure-vessel Mechanical design, and multidisciplinary reasoning. Long-distance pipeline engineering is not absorbed. The repository has no separate canonical pipeline-engineering discipline. The discipline roadmap names D5 as PIPING. Until a later canonical project architecture explicitly includes long-distance pipeline engineering, D5 does not invent that scope.

## Object taxonomy

Adopted object types are PIPING_SYSTEM_CONTEXT, PIPING_LINE, PIPING_SERVICE_CONTEXT, PIPING_DESIGN_CONDITION_CONTEXT, PIPING_SPECIFICATION_CONTEXT, PIPING_COMPONENT_CONTEXT, PIPING_VALVE_CONTEXT, PIPING_SPECIALTY_CONTEXT, PIPING_CONNECTION_CONTEXT, PIPING_ROUTE_CONTEXT, PIPING_SUPPORT_REQUIREMENT_CONTEXT, PIPING_STRESS_FLEXIBILITY_CONTEXT, PIPING_FABRICATION_CONTEXT, PIPING_TEST_INSPECTION_CONTEXT, PIPING_INTERFACE_POINT, and PIPING_GOVERNANCE_RECORD. Requirement, assumption, decision, and evidence reference are governed record types beside that taxonomy. They do not create a second object model.

Component kinds such as pipe, elbow, tee, reducer, flange, valve, strainer, expansion joint, flexible connection, specialty, branch connection, and fitting are classifications under component or valve context. They are not extra object types created to mirror drawings. An isometric or spool document is fabrication context, not a competing identity. An unknown or freeform type does not become authoritative by being named.

## Identity

Each adopted object will have a stable identity inside tenant, workspace, and project scope, a canonical object type, a line number or tag where one applies, a service or function, a lifecycle or maturity stage, an exact revision, explicit relationships, provenance, canonical currentness, a canonical fingerprint, and immutable history. Identity reuses canonical EOS identity governance. No parallel identity system, currentness engine, digital thread, or fingerprint algorithm is created. Object identity is not the engineering content of the object and is not validated engineering.

## System, line, and service

A future phase may govern system, line, service, line number or tag, line and system membership, equipment connection, branch and connection, service and function, and Piping ownership. A line identity is not a Process stream. A service context is not a Process design. Line or system membership is not inferred from tag similarity, document co-occurrence, filename, or semantic similarity. Relationships must be explicit governed relationships.

## Process boundary

D6 remains future Process authority. D5 may later cite an exact Process source. A Process condition is not a Piping design condition. D5 does not create an authoritative Process condition or Process design. A Process condition may become a basis for a Piping design condition only through explicit governed adoption. There is no automatic promotion.

## Design conditions and specifications

Future governed context may record design pressure, design temperature, operating pressure, operating temperature, vacuum, transient or design-case context, test condition, corrosion or erosion allowance, and design-life context, plus other Piping-owned design criteria that are explicitly supplied. A source condition is not an adopted Piping design condition. A Piping design condition is not a validated Piping design. This phase derives no values.

Specification context may later record piping material specification, class or spec, material designation, pressure-rating context, flange-rating context, wall or schedule context, corrosion allowance, component-class applicability, service applicability, and standards or code references. A specification reference is not code conformance. A piping class is not a validated material selection. There is no code-compliance calculation and no automated material selection.

## Components and connections

A component being present is not engineering validation. D4 remains equipment authority. D5 may later own Piping-side connection truth: nozzle or interface reference, connection point, orientation, connection size and rating context, allowable-load reference, package boundary, and Piping-side connection requirement. A Mechanical nozzle context is not Piping design. A Piping connection context is not Mechanical equipment design. Authority is not transferred.

The D5 to D4 contract reuses the D4 future Piping subjects for nozzle or interface points, allowable nozzle-load references, connection geometry, and equipment connection requirements, and adds nozzle orientation, package piping boundary, equipment movement or displacement context, and maintenance or access constraints relevant to Piping. That is the same contract extended, not a competing one.

## Routing, supports, and stress

Routing context may later record a route segment, routing constraint, slope requirement, elevation context, equipment connection, access constraint, maintenance-clearance reference, drain or vent requirement, underground or above-ground classification, and constructability constraint. A route context is not clash-free, not constructable, and not optimized. Spatial clash detection, route optimization, and automatic routing stay at count zero.

Support and restraint context may later record a support location requirement, restraint type, guide or anchor requirement, spring or support requirement, load or result reference, support function, and movement context. A Piping support requirement is not Structural support design and is not structural adequacy. D1 remains Structural authority for structural design. D5 does not transfer Structural authority. D1 remains incomplete and is not reopened.

Stress and flexibility context may later record analysis-case identity, load-case context, displacement context, restraint context, calculated-result evidence, allowable or reference context, analysis provenance, software or tool evidence, limitations, and validation state. Stress calculation, flexibility calculation, and support-load calculation stay at count zero. A stress-analysis result is not a validated Piping design and is not Structural support validation.

## Insulation, fabrication, and testing

Piping-owned context may later record an insulation requirement, coating or lining, heat-tracing requirement, personnel protection, and corrosion-protection context. A heat-tracing requirement is not Electrical design. D7 remains Electrical authority.

Fabrication context may later record isometric context, spool context, fabrication status, weld or joint identity, field or shop classification, fabrication evidence, revision and currentness, and inspection or test association. An isometric is not Piping identity. A spool document is not engineering-validated.

Inspection and test context may later record an inspection requirement, NDE requirement, weld examination, hydrotest, pneumatic or leak test, test pack, test evidence, result or status, and certificate or evidence. A test requirement is not a completed test. A completed test is not engineering validation. An NDE result is not code conformance. Evidence is not validation. An observation is not an engineering interpretation. A source requirement is not an adopted Piping requirement. Vendor data is not Piping engineering adoption and is not engineering-validated.

## Governance, review, and currentness

Requirements, assumptions, decisions, and change reuse canonical EOS governance. No parallel requirement, assumption, decision, or change engine is created. Review and validation reuse canonical review governance: exact subject revision, reviewer authority, request, findings, disposition, completion, validation basis, validation state, reassessment link, and history. AI is not Piping engineering authority and is not Piping reviewer authority. An administrator is neither. Reviewed is not validated. Validated is not conformant, approved, or certified. AI must not invent an authoritative Process condition, Piping design condition, or Piping requirement, must not infer authoritative lineage, and must not auto-validate Piping engineering. An AI suggestion may exist only as an explicitly non-authoritative candidate.

Currentness, digital thread, and fingerprint reuse the canonical mechanisms already used by D4. Explicit lineage only. Historical references are not auto-repointed.

## Discipline interfaces and D9

Future contracts are defined toward D1 for support reactions and loads, restraint requirements, anchor loads, imposed-load context, and structural penetration or support requirements; toward D2 for underground corridors, buried piping, trenches, crossings, drainage interaction, site-routing constraints, and civil support or encasement; toward D3 for buried-pipe ground-interaction source context, settlement-related Piping requirements, ground-condition references, and trench geotechnical sources, without creating Geotechnical conclusions; toward D6 for Process conditions, fluid or service identity, stream source, design cases, line-sizing source or result references, relief blowdown drain or vent requirements, and Process requirements that drive Piping context; toward D7 for electric heat-tracing requirements, bonding or earthing context, electrically actuated valves, and package interfaces; and toward D8 for control-valve and instrument connections, inline instruments, tappings, actuated valves, measurement connections, and package instrumentation boundaries. An instrument connection context is not Instrumentation and Control design.

One future D5 source contract may expose governed identity, system and line context, service, design conditions, specifications and classes, component context, routing constraints, support and restraint context, stress and flexibility evidence, fabrication and testing evidence, requirements assumptions decisions and change, interfaces, review and validation, currentness, lineage, authority, limitations, provenance, and fingerprint. The source-contract runtime count is zero. It does not create a new engineering fact and does not transfer engineering authority. Cross-discipline reasoning, conflict detection, conflict resolution, and recommendation stay at count zero. D6, D7, D8, and D9 runtime stay unstarted. D9 count stays zero.

Pipe sizing, hydraulic calculation, pressure drop, wall thickness, branch reinforcement, flange rating, piping stress, flexibility, support load, route optimization, automatic routing, spatial clash detection, automatic redesign, automatic validation, conformance, approval, and certification stay at count zero. No hidden calculation sits under an architecture label.

## Phase decomposition

The canonical implementation sequence has twelve phases: Piping identity and object foundation, system line and service context, design conditions and specification context, component connection and valve context, routing arrangement and fabrication context, stress support and test evidence context, requirements assumptions and change, discipline interfaces and handoffs, review and validation governance, currentness and digital thread, the D9 source contract, and Piping bounded closeout. R1 later implements only governed Piping identities and the canonical object taxonomy with scope, lifecycle, provenance, currentness, and fingerprint. It does not implement line or service technical context, design conditions, specifications, components, routing, stress or support, review, interfaces, the D9 source contract, or calculations. This phase does not execute R1.
